Lesson 01 / The basics

What makes an insect an insect?

An insect is a small invertebrate animal with a three-part body, six jointed legs, and one pair of antennae. Many adults also have wings.

🔍 Observe the body plan

Grasshopper anatomy — use the labels to identify its body structures.

Meet the insect body

👀
Head

Contains the eyes, antennae, and mouthparts. It helps the insect sense its surroundings and feed.

🦵
Thorax

The movement centre. All six legs and, when present, the wings are attached here.

🌾
Abdomen

Contains many digestive and reproductive organs, along with openings used for breathing.

Nature detective tip: Count the legs first! Spiders have eight legs and belong to the arachnids, not the insects.

Lesson 03 / Featured order

The magical world of Lepidoptera

The order Lepidoptera includes butterflies and moths. Its name comes from Greek roots meaning scale and wing — a beautiful clue to what makes them special.

🦋 180,000+ described species

Butterflies and moths share scaled wings, but often differ in their antennae, activity patterns and resting posture.

✓

Wings covered in scales

Two pairs of membranous wings carry thousands of overlapping tiny scales, creating colour, patterns and protection.

✓

A coiled feeding tube

Many adults have a flexible proboscis that uncoils to sip nectar, water or other liquids.

✓

Sensitive eyes and antennae

Compound eyes detect their surroundings. Antennae help sense odours, including scents used to find mates.

✓

Complete metamorphosis

They transform dramatically through four stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult.

Did you know? A moth is not simply a brown butterfly. Some moths are brightly coloured and active in daylight!

Lesson 04 / Our living planet

Why insects matter

These small creatures help keep ecosystems working and affect farming, food, forests and human health.

🌼

Pollination

Bees, butterflies and other insects carry pollen between flowers, helping many plants produce fruits and seeds.

🐞

Natural pest control

Ladybird beetles and parasitoid wasps naturally help reduce some crop pests. Spiders also help, though they are not insects.

🌱

Nutrient recycling

Decomposer insects break down dead organic matter, helping return nutrients to soil ecosystems.

🐦

Food webs

Insects and caterpillars provide essential food for birds, frogs, fish and many other animals.

🍯

Useful products

Honeybees make honey and beeswax. The silkworm moth Bombyx mori is used in silk production.

🌾

Farming and health

Some caterpillars damage crops; mosquitoes can spread disease. Studying insects supports agriculture and public health.
